For a customer, friction appears when products are hard to compare, saved choices disappear, or order updates are unclear. For a company, the same journey creates different questions: What is selling? Which orders need action? What stock is running low?

OptiCart connects these needs through shared product, customer, and order information. That gives the buyer continuity, while staff receive the controls and visibility that match their responsibilities. Mahmod will show how those two sides work in practice.

03SLIDE
MMahmod

Make buying easier to complete

Show the implemented end-to-end customer journey and explain its commercial value.

0:3472 words
EXACT SCRIPT

On the customer side, OptiCart supports the full buying path. Visitors can browse categories, search and filter products, compare variants, read verified-purchase reviews, and receive recommendations. A guest can save a wishlist or build a cart, then keep that work when signing in.

Coupons, checkout, order history, status notifications, and feedback continue the journey after the product page. The business value is straightforward: fewer unnecessary breaks between interest, purchase, and ongoing service.

Turn demand into coordinated action

Demonstrate operational control across orders, stock, procurement, roles, and accountability.

EXACT SCRIPT

The same order becomes coordinated work for the business. Staff can manage the catalog and variants, move orders through fulfillment, and communicate status changes to customers. Low-stock thresholds create alerts before availability becomes a surprise.

Recording a supplier purchase increases inventory and captures procurement cost in the same workflow. Different roles receive appropriate controls, while audit logs provide accountability for administrative actions. Haya will now show how this operational data supports better decisions.
